## Slideshow Script: College Affordability Dashboard Evaluation

This script provides a slide-by-slide guide for your presentation, complete with narration and visual suggestions.

---

### **Slide 1: Title Slide**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A clean, professional title slide.
    *   Use a background image that is relevant but not distracting (e.g., a subtle photo of a university campus, a graduation cap, or an abstract data visualization).
    *   Your dashboard's logo or a key chart from it, if available.

*   **Script:**
    > "Good morning/afternoon, everyone. My name is [Your Name], and today I'll be presenting the results of a usability study for my term project, the College Affordability Dashboard. This project explores one of the most significant financial challenges facing students and families today."

---

### **Slide 2: Dashboard Overview & Goals**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A clean, full-screen screenshot of your dashboard's main landing page.
    *   Use arrows or callout boxes to highlight 1-2 key features (e.g., the main chart, filter controls).

*   **Script:**
    > "The goal of the College Affordability Dashboard is to demystify the true cost of higher education in the United States. It's designed to help prospective students, families, and policymakers explore trends in tuition, debt, and the return on investment for different degrees."
    >
    > "The central problem it addresses is 'sticker shock'—the gap between the advertised tuition and the actual net price after aid. It helps users answer questions like: How has the cost of college changed over time? And which majors offer the best financial returns?"
    >
    > "Our target audience includes high school students planning for college, parents supporting them, and educational advisors."

---

### **Slide 3: Usability Study: Methodology**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   Use simple, clean icons to represent each point (e.g., a speech bubble for 'Think-Aloud', a group icon for 'Participants', a checklist for 'Procedure').
    *   Keep the slide text minimal and clear.

*   **Script:**
    > "To evaluate the dashboard's effectiveness, we conducted a think-aloud usability study with five participants. Before they began, I explained the dashboard's purpose. During the sessions, I asked each user to explore the interface while verbalizing their thought process—what they were looking for, what they found confusing, and what they found insightful. My role was purely observational, to capture their authentic, unfiltered experience."

---

### **Slide 4: Participant Demographics**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A simple table or a series of illustrated personas to represent the user groups.
    *   Avoid stock photos; use icons or simple silhouettes to maintain anonymity.

*   **Script:**
    > "Our test group was diverse to ensure a wide range of perspectives. It included:
    > *   A current computer science student who is familiar with data tools.
    *   A graphic designer with a background in user experience.
    *   A family member with limited technical expertise.
    *   A classmate from a non-technical major.
    *   And a high school guidance counselor.
    > This mix allowed us to see how both technical and non-technical users would interact with the dashboard."

---

### **Slide 5-9: Key Observations (1 Slide Per User)**

*   **Visuals (for each slide):**
    *   Structure as three columns: Strengths, Challenges, and Key Quotes.
    *   Use icons (e.g., a checkmark for strengths, a warning sign for challenges) to visually separate the feedback.
    *   Include a small, anonymous persona icon to represent the user.
    *   **Crucially, use a screenshot of the specific part of the dashboard the user was interacting with** to illustrate their point (e.g., a screenshot of the confusing filter panel).

*   **Script (Example for one user):**
    > **Slide 5: User 1 - The CS Student**
    > "Our first user, the computer science student, immediately understood the data visualizations and appreciated the interactive elements. They found the historical tuition trend chart very intuitive."
    >
    > "However, they struggled with the filtering logic, noting that selecting multiple states didn't combine the data as expected. They also pointed out a minor bug where the chart tooltip would sometimes flicker."
    >
    > "A direct quote that captured their experience was: 'I see what the chart is showing, but I'm not sure how to isolate the data for public vs. private schools in just the Midwest.'"

    *(Repeat this structure for Slides 6, 7, 8, and 9, tailoring the content for each user.)*

---

### **Slide 10: Summary of Lessons Learned**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A summary graphic, perhaps a quadrant chart with "Key Strengths," "Primary Weaknesses," "Common Themes," and "Surprising Insights."
    *   Use large, readable text to highlight the main takeaways.

*   **Script:**
    > "Across the five sessions, several key themes emerged.
    > *   **Common Theme:** Nearly all users found the main tuition trend chart to be the most compelling feature. However, three of the five users struggled to locate or effectively use the advanced filter panel on their first try.
    *   **Key Strength:** The dashboard was consistently praised for its clean design and the clarity of its primary visualizations.
    *   **Primary Weakness:** The biggest usability hurdle was the lack of clear instructions or labels for the filtering and comparison features.
    *   **Surprising Insight:** I was surprised to learn that two users wanted to download the raw data, a feature I hadn't initially considered essential."

---

### **Slide 11: Proposed Improvements & Design Changes**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A "Before & After" mockup is highly effective here. Show a screenshot of the current problematic area next to a redesigned version.
    *   Alternatively, use a prioritized list (High, Medium, Low Priority) with clear descriptions.

*   **Script:**
    > "Based on this feedback, I've developed a list of proposed improvements.
    > *   **High Priority:** First, I plan to redesign the filter panel, making it always visible and using clearer labels. For example, changing 'Type' to 'School Type (Public/Private)'.
    *   **UI/UX Enhancement:** I will also add a 'Reset Filters' button, which was a common request, and provide a short, introductory text block explaining how to use the interactive features.
    *   **New Feature:** Finally, based on user feedback, I will add a 'Download Data (CSV)' button to the main chart."

---

### **Slide 12: Empathy Map (Optional but Recommended)**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A large, clear Empathy Map diagram with the four quadrants (Says, Thinks, Does, Feels).
    *   Use sticky-note-style text boxes to populate each quadrant with summary points from the user sessions.

*   **Script:**
    > "To better synthesize the user experience, I created an empathy map.
    > *   **Says:** We heard things like, 'This is more expensive than I thought,' and 'How do I filter this?'
    *   **Thinks:** Users were often wondering, 'Is this number for just one year?' or feeling 'This is overwhelming.'
    *   **Does:** We observed users repeatedly clicking on different states and trying to drag the time-slider.
    *   **Feels:** The dominant emotions were initial interest, followed by confusion when filters didn't work as expected, and finally insight when they understood the data.
    > This exercise helped translate user feedback into a deeper understanding of their needs and frustrations."

---

### **Slide 13: List of Dashboards Evaluated**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A simple, clean list.
    *   Include the title of each dashboard you evaluated and perhaps the name of the student who created it (if appropriate).

*   **Script:**
    > "As part of this course, I also had the opportunity to evaluate several of my peers' dashboards, which was a valuable learning experience. The dashboards I evaluated were [List the names of the dashboards, e.g., 'Antibiotic Effectiveness Analysis,' 'SF AirBnb Listings,' etc.]."

---

### **Slide 14: Conclusion & Thank You**

*   **Visuals:**
    *   A simple, clean slide with your key message.
    *   Include your name and contact information (email, portfolio link).

*   **Script:**
    > "In conclusion, this usability study was incredibly valuable. It highlighted that while the core visualizations of the dashboard are effective, the user experience can be significantly improved by refining the interactive controls and providing clearer guidance."
    >
    > "Thank you for your time. I'd now be happy to answer any questions you may have."
