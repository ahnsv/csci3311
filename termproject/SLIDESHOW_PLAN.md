## Slideshow Report Plan for Term Project Evaluation

This document outlines the structure and content for the term project evaluation slideshow, based on the requirements in `EVAL.md`.

---

### Slideshow Structure

**Slide 1: Title Slide**
*   **Title:** Term Project Evaluation: [Your Dashboard's Name]
*   **Author(s):** [Your Name(s)]
*   **Course:** CSCI 3311
*   **Date:** [Date of Submission]

**Slide 2: Dashboard Overview & Goals**
*   **Purpose:** Briefly explain what your dashboard does.
*   **Problem Solved:** What question does it answer or what problem does it help users explore?
*   **Target Audience:** Who is this dashboard designed for?

**Slide 3: Usability Study: Methodology**
*   **Method Used:** Think-Aloud Usability Study.
*   **Number of Participants:** 5 Users.
*   **Procedure:**
    *   Briefed users on the dashboard's goal.
    *   Asked users to verbalize their thoughts, actions, and impressions while exploring.
    *   Observed and took notes without providing assistance.

**Slide 4: Participant Demographics**
*   Provide a brief, anonymous description of each participant.
*   **Example:**
    *   **User 1:** Computer Science student, frequent user of data analysis tools.
    *   **User 2:** Graphic designer with a strong background in UI/UX principles.
    *   **User 3:** Family member with limited exposure to complex dashboards.
    *   **User 4:** Classmate from a non-technical major.
    *   **User 5:** ...
*   *Note: Do not use names or any other personally identifiable information.*

**Slide 5-9: Key Observations (1 Slide Per User)**
*   **User [1-5]:**
    *   **Strengths (What worked well):** What did they understand immediately? What did they find helpful or intuitive?
    *   **Challenges (What was difficult):** Where did they struggle? What features were hard to find or use?
    *   **Points of Confusion (What was unclear):** Which labels, charts, or interactions were confusing?
    *   **Direct Quotes:** (Optional, but highly effective) e.g., "I expected this button to do X, but it did Y."

**Slide 10: Summary of Lessons Learned**
*   Synthesize the feedback from all five sessions.
*   **Common Themes:** What patterns emerged across multiple users?
    *   *Example: "Three out of five users had trouble locating the filter panel."*
*   **Key Strengths:** What aspects of the dashboard were consistently praised?
*   **Primary Weaknesses:** What were the most significant usability hurdles?
*   **Surprising Insights:** Were there any unexpected findings?

**Slide 11: Proposed Improvements & Design Changes**
*   Present a clear, actionable list of changes based on your findings.
*   Categorize for clarity:
    *   **High-Priority Fixes:** e.g., "Clarify the label on the main chart from 'Value' to 'Average Price'."
    *   **UI/UX Enhancements:** e.g., "Add a 'Reset Filters' button."
    *   **New Feature Suggestions:** e.g., "Implement a data export feature as requested by two users."

**Slide 12: Empathy Map (Recommended)**
*   Visually organize user feedback into four quadrants to build a deeper understanding of their experience.
    *   **Says:** Key quotes.
    *   **Thinks:** What users were likely thinking.
    *   **Does:** Actions they took (e.g., clicking, scrolling).
    *   **Feels:** Their emotional response (e.g., confused, interested, frustrated).

**Slide 13: List of Dashboards Evaluated**
*   List the names or topics of the other student dashboards you evaluated as part of the in-class activity.

**Slide 14: Conclusion**
*   Briefly summarize the value of the feedback.
*   Thank the audience and open the floor for questions.
