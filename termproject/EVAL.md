## Assignment Topic
Term Project Evaluation

## Purpose
This stage lets you step into the shoes of your audience. A think-aloud study helps reveal unexpected usability challenges and surface moments of insight or confusion. You'll learn whether your dashboard supports intuitive interaction and meaningful exploration.

## Description
Work with other students in class to conduct a think-aloud usability study on your dashboard. Each project must test its dashboard with at least five users.

- Explain the goal of the dashboard to each user before they begin.
- During the session, ask users to explore your dashboard while thinking aloud—sharing what they’re doing, looking for, or struggling with.
- Avoid helping them as they interact. Let them talk through confusion or discovery on their own.
- Your goal is to observe, reflect, and report on what you learn, not necessarily to implement changes yet.
- If you're unable to work with other students in the same class, it's acceptable to include family and friends as evaluation participants.

## Directions 
Submit a slideshow report (PDF format) that includes:

- A brief description of who you tested with (e.g., demographics)—avoid using identifiable information like names
- Key observations from each session (what users struggled with, understood easily, found helpful, or unclear)
- A summary of lessons learned across the five users
- A list of proposed improvements or design changes based on what you observed
- List the dashboards you evaluated.
- (Optional but recommended) Use an empathy mapLinks to an external site. to organize user feedback (what users say, think, do, and feel)
